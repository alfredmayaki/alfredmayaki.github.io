Milano Cortina 2026

🗓️ 6 — 22 February 2026

The XXV Olympic Winter Games return to Italy for the first time since Turin 2006. Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo will host the world's greatest winter athletes across the Alps and the Lombardy plain.

116 Medal Events
16 Sports
~2,900 Athletes
90+ Countries

Key Dates

  • 6 Feb

    Opening Ceremony

    Stadio Giuseppe Meazza (San Siro), Milan — the first outdoor Olympic opening ceremony in a football stadium in Winter Games history.

  • 7 Feb

    First Medal Events

    Competition begins across biathlon, cross-country skiing, short track speed skating, freestyle skiing, and ski jumping.

  • 8 Feb

    Alpine Skiing Starts

    The giant slalom kicks off alpine action at Cortina d'Ampezzo, returning to where the 1956 Winter Games were held.

  • 11 Feb

    Figure Skating Team Event Final

    One of the most-watched events of any Winter Olympics — team competition concludes early in the programme.

  • 20 Feb

    Ice Hockey Finals Begin

    Medal rounds in ice hockey at the PalaItalia Santa Giulia arena in Milan.

  • 22 Feb

    Closing Ceremony

    Arena di Verona — the iconic Roman amphitheatre hosts the closing ceremony, passing the flag to the 2030 French Alps hosts.

Athletes to Watch

🇺🇸

Mikaela Shiffrin

Alpine Skiing

The most decorated World Cup alpine skier in history with 100+ victories. Seeking to add to her 3 Olympic medals and cement her legacy as the greatest slalom racer ever.

🇳🇴

Johannes Høsflot Klæbo

Cross-Country Skiing

Norway's sprint king with 4 Olympic golds (including 3 at Beijing 2022). Aiming to become the most decorated male cross-country skier in Olympic history at age 29.

🇺🇸

Chloe Kim

Snowboard Halfpipe

Back-to-back Olympic champion in halfpipe (2018 & 2022). The dominant force in women's snowboarding pursues a historic three-peat on Italian snow.

🇨🇳

Eileen Gu

Freestyle Skiing

Triple medallist at Beijing 2022 (2 golds, 1 silver). The Stanford-educated freeskier is among the most marketable athletes in winter sports.

🇳🇱

Jutta Leerdam

Speed Skating

Dutch speed skating star and world champion. The Netherlands' medal machine aims to continue their dominance on the long track oval.

🇯🇵

Yuzuru Hanyu

Figure Skating (Legacy)

Two-time Olympic champion turned professional. While retired from competitive skating, his legacy defines the sport heading into Milano Cortina.

🇩🇪

Francesco Friedrich

Bobsled

Germany's dominant bobsled pilot with 4 consecutive Olympic golds (2- and 4-man). Seeks to extend his unbeaten Olympic streak in the sliding centre.

🇮🇹

Sofia Goggia

Alpine Skiing

Italy's downhill queen and home crowd favourite. The 2018 Olympic champion is hungry to deliver gold on home soil in Cortina.

🇫🇷

Quentin Fillon Maillet

Biathlon

France's biathlon superstar who claimed 5 medals at Beijing 2022. One of the most versatile winter athletes competing across sprint, pursuit, and relay.

Records That Could Fall

🥇

Marit Bjørgen's 15 Olympic Medals

The Norwegian cross-country legend holds the all-time Winter Olympic medal record. Her compatriots Klæbo and Therese Johaug's successors are closing in.

⏱️

Speed Skating World Records

The Baselga di Piné oval sits at ~1,000m elevation — thinner air could produce blistering times in long-track events, threatening records set at higher-altitude venues.

🏂

Chloe Kim — First Three-Peat in Halfpipe

No snowboarder has ever won three consecutive Olympic gold medals in halfpipe. Kim can make history in Livigno.

⛷️

Shiffrin's All-Time Alpine Medal Count

With 3 Olympic medals already, Shiffrin could surpass the record for most Olympic medals by a US alpine skier across her slalom, giant slalom, and super-G entries.

🇳🇴

Norway's All-Time Winter Medal Tally

Norway leads all nations with 400+ Winter Olympic medals. Milano Cortina 2026 will likely see them extend this commanding lead further.

🆕

First-Ever Ski Mountaineering Medals

Ski mountaineering debuts as an Olympic sport in 2026. The first gold medallists in sprint, individual, and mixed relay will write themselves into the record books.

Venue Clusters

🏟️

Milan

Opening ceremony at San Siro. Ice hockey, figure skating, speed skating, and short track at PalaItalia and Milan Forum.

🏔️

Cortina d'Ampezzo

Alpine skiing, bobsled, luge, skeleton, and curling. The "Queen of the Dolomites" hosted the 1956 Winter Games.

🎿

Livigno

Freestyle skiing and snowboard events in the duty-free alpine town near the Swiss border, at 1,816m elevation.

❄️

Anterselva / Antholz

Biathlon centre in South Tyrol, one of the world's premier biathlon venues at 1,600m altitude.

⛸️

Baselga di Piné

Long-track speed skating at the new Ice Oval. Situated in the Trentino mountains at ~1,000m elevation.

🏛️

Verona

The Arena di Verona, a 1st-century Roman amphitheatre, hosts the closing ceremony — the oldest Olympic ceremony venue ever.

🏕️

Predazzo & Val di Fiemme

Ski jumping, Nordic combined, and cross-country skiing in the Fiemme Valley of Trentino — a proven Nordic sports centre.

🏔️

Bormio

Ski mountaineering (Olympic debut) and additional alpine events on the legendary Stelvio course at 2,255m.

Did You Know?

🇮🇹

Italy's 4th Olympics

Italy has hosted the Olympics four times: Rome 1960 (Summer), Cortina 1956 (Winter), Turin 2006 (Winter), and now Milano Cortina 2026. Cortina is the first city to host Winter Games twice.

🏟️

San Siro's Farewell

The opening ceremony at Stadio San Siro may be among the last major events at the legendary 80,000-seat stadium, with plans for its redevelopment ongoing.

🏛️

2,000-Year-Old Venue

The closing ceremony at the Arena di Verona makes it the oldest structure ever used for an Olympic ceremony — built in 30 AD during the Roman Empire.

🧗

Ski Mountaineering Debut

Ski mountaineering (skimo) joins the Olympic programme for the first time in 2026, with 3 medal events. Athletes race uphill and downhill on skis in extreme alpine terrain.

🌍

Sustainability Focus

Milano Cortina 2026 aims to use 93% existing or temporary venues, the highest proportion in Winter Games history. The organisers target carbon-neutral operations.

🎭

The Mascots — Tina & Milo

The official mascots are two stoats (ermines) named Tina and Milo. They represent the alpine environment and were the first Olympic mascots chosen through a public vote by Italian school children.
