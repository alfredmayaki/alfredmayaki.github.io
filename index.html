Collective Commonality & Tactical Polyvalence

How the Public Sector Can Design Itself to Mitigate Industrial Disputes and Optimise Social Justice

InstitutionThe Open University
Year2026
FormatDissertation
JELJ52 Â· J45 Â· C72

A study of bargaining, welfare and democratic participation.

The dissertation brings together signalling theory and actor-network theory to examine collective bargaining in the public sector. It uses the British Social Attitudes Survey, Labour Force Survey and OECD/AIAS ICTWSS data, applying two-way fixed-effects regression to explore the relationship between union rights, wage growth and welfare expenditure.

Its central argument is that collective bargaining is not only an industrial-relations mechanism, but a democratic institution.
